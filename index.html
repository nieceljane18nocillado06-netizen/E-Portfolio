My Reflection

⟡

I will be honest — I was not the kind of person who walked into this subject with a deep love for art. Art was never really my world. I was a BSIT student, more comfortable with screens and logic than with paintings and principles. When I first saw the course outline for GE Elec 6, the words visual arts, art movements, and cinematographic techniques did not exactly excite me. It felt like a subject I would simply have to get through.

But getting through it turned out to be something else entirely. The early weeks were a genuine eye-opener. I had never thought to ask why we study art, or what it even means to read something visual rather than simply look at it. Learning the distinction between an author's meaning and an audience's meaning in visual analysis made me realize that art is never passive — it asks something of the person in front of it.

The elements and principles of art, which I had initially dismissed as things I would memorize and forget, quietly became a framework I started applying without thinking about it — not just in class, but in the way I noticed things around me. A photograph. A street mural. The framing of a film shot. Suddenly, I had language for what I was seeing.

What surprised me most was the social dimension of visual arts — the lessons on social consciousness and the purposes art serves in a community. I had always thought of art as decoration, as something beautiful but ultimately ornamental. Studying Joey Velasco's Hapag ng Pag-asa completely dismantled that assumption.

Those lessons showed me that art can be an act of protest, of witness, of love — that a single painting could hold the lives of real, forgotten people and demand that the world see them. The final term broadened that understanding even further. Moving through Photography, Film, Cinema, and Theater in our reporting weeks, it became clear that every visual art form has its own language, its own grammar, its own way of making meaning.

I had watched films my whole life without ever thinking about cinematographic techniques or aesthetic factors. I had sat in audiences without understanding the invisible architecture of what made a performance work. The deeper the course went, the more I realized how much I had been missing — not because I lacked intelligence, but because I had never been given the vocabulary to see.

I will not pretend this subject changed everything overnight. But it did something quieter and more lasting — it taught me how to pay attention. I left GE Elec 6 not as someone who suddenly loves art, but as someone who finally understands why it matters. That, I think, is a more honest and more durable thing to take away. The appreciation came after the lessons, and in some ways, that makes it feel more truly mine.

"Art enables us to find ourselves and lose ourselves at the same time." — Thomas Merton

Quiz

✦ Score: 8 / 10

✍ My Reflection — Quiz

Looking back at this quiz, an 8 out of 10 is something I am genuinely proud of, even if a small part of me wished those last two points had gone differently. The quiz covered fundamental concepts in visual arts — things like medium, technique, curation, and the various forms and methods that artists use to create their work — and while most of it came back to me naturally, the two items I missed were a quiet reminder that some concepts had slipped through the cracks between lessons and memory. It was humbling in the best way; I realized that art is a much wider vocabulary than I had given it credit for, and that knowing a few terms is not the same as truly understanding the language. Still, scoring 8 out of 10 on the very first quiz gave me a sense that I was starting the subject on solid ground, and those two missed answers became less of a disappointment and more of a nudge — a signal to pay closer attention, review more carefully, and not take for granted the details that seem small but carry real meaning in the study of visual arts.

long quiz · March 6, 2026

Summative / Long Quiz

✦ Score: 27 / 30

✍ My Reflection — Summative

This long quiz is one I look back on with a quiet sense of satisfaction — not just because of the score itself, but because of what the preparation behind it taught me. The quiz spanned a wide range of visual arts concepts: from matching theories like the Auteur approach and Iconographic analysis, to identifying art forms such as installation art and industrial design, all the way to enumerating the types of medium, elements, and principles of art. What I did not expect was that something as seemingly simple as following the instructions carefully would reflect the very values the course had been teaching — that discipline and attention to detail are never wasted, and that in academics, just as in art, how you present your work matters just as much as what you put into it. Following instructions is not just about compliance; it is a form of respect for the craft, and this exam proved that such care, however quiet and unglamorous, always finds its reward.

Reporting — Cinema

✦ GE Elec 6 · Finals Period

✍ My Reflection — Reporting

I was the one who introduced our group's topic on Cinema — covering its etymology, its definition, and the foundational nature of film as both artistic expression and technological innovation. When our class shifted to an online setup, our group did not treat it as a reason to deliver any less. The word "cinema" itself — from the Greek kinema, meaning "movement" — reframed the entire topic for me. Cinema has always been, at its core, about the illusion of motion and the act of witnessing, and even in an online classroom, that relationship between presenter and audience still held. I was proud of how our presentation conveyed the idea that cinema reflects human experiences, cultural values, and social realities — that it is never simply entertainment, but a mirror held up to the world. The experience reminded me that connection, like cinema, does not require a physical screen to be real.

A Letter of Gratitude

✦

From Student to Instructor

Dear Ma'am Jhonna,

Good day!

Spending this semester with you in GE Elec 6 has been one of the more quietly meaningful experiences of my second year. Even on the days when our meetings were fewer than expected, the lessons you prepared left a lasting impression — not just on what I know, but on how I see.

I came into this subject certain that visual arts was not for someone like me — a BSIT student more comfortable with code and logic than canvases and compositions. What I did not expect was how gently and persistently the subject would dismantle that certainty. You taught us not just the elements and principles of art, but the deeper truth behind them: that art is a form of attention, a way of being present to the world, and a language that speaks even when words fall short.

"I would like to thank you not only for your teaching but also for the compassion and consideration you showed our class this semester. You have helped us in more ways than you may have realized, and we are genuinely grateful."

The lessons on social consciousness in visual arts — particularly studying Joey Velasco's work — reminded me that beauty and purpose are not opposites, and that art at its most honest is never decoration but witness. I carry that lesson beyond this subject.

Learning Reading Visual Arts with you was one of the good things that happened this semester. Thank you for opening a door I did not know I needed to walk through.

With love and respect,

Niecel Jane C. Nocillado ♥
